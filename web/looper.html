Ideas for loops that go somewhere
  1. Build from the bottom. Track 1: a bass line on the low strings, palm-muted. Or play a phrase on the high strings and switch it to ½×: it drops an octave and plays half as fast, a slow, deep line under everything. Track 2: chords. Track 3: a riff or melody. Keep track 4 for solos you can undo.
  2. Leave space. Two bars of chords and two of silence makes room for a call and response with yourself.
  3. Polymeter. Record a 3-bar loop over a 4-bar one. They come round together every 12 bars, so the music keeps changing while every loop repeats.
  4. Reverse swells. Record a few ringing notes with lots of Room, then Rev: they swell in like a bowed instrument. Over a forward loop of the same chords, it sounds like an orchestra.
  5. Evolving loops. Set Fade to 75% and overdub for a while: each pass, the old layers drop by a quarter, so the loop slowly turns into what you're playing now.
  6. ×2, then change the second half. Double a 2-bar loop and overdub only over bars 3–4: a 4-bar phrase that answers itself.
  7. Solo over it. The card above names the key. Start in box 1 of the scale it suggests, then try the other mode it names: same root, a different colour.
How it works

The loops are recorded and played in an AudioWorklet, so every loop wraps on an exact sample, and loops of different lengths never drift apart. In Tempo mode a recording starts and stops on a bar line: a press up to 0.15 s after the line still counts as on it, because the page keeps the last few seconds of input. Overdubs start and stop exactly when you press. The loop's seam is crossfaded (3 ms), and so is every overdub's start and end, and every switch of reverse or speed, so nothing clicks. Each overdub is a layer of its own, so Undo takes off the last one (and Redo puts it back). With Fade below 100%, each pass of an overdub is its own layer, and the older ones fade by that much each time round. The loop engine follows SooperLooper's design. The key comes from the notes in all the loops (their pitch-class profile), matched against Krumhansl and Kessler's key profiles. See the README for sources.
