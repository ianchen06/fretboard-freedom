      How it works

      Every attack the input hears (a strum, a chuck, a palm-muted chug) is timed to about a millisecond and lined up with the nearest step of the pattern. A hit on a step that should sound counts; one on a ghost stroke or in a rest is an extra; a step with nothing near it is missed. Timing is your offset from that step, after the timing offset on the right is taken off.

      Clean pass

      At least 95% of the hits, at most one extra, on average within ±20 ms of the beat, and a spread under ±20 ms. With the tempo ladder on, clean passes in a row move the tempo up about 4%; two rough ones move it down.

      Practice tips
      1. 1
        Measure the timing offset once per setup (input and output device), or every hit reads early or late by the same amount.
      2. 2
        Start slower than you think. Clean at 70 beats sloppy at 100. The ladder will move you up.
      3. 3
        Count out loud and tap your foot. Your body is the clock the gap click tests.
      4. 4
        Headphones or an interface. A mic that hears the click counts it as your hits.
