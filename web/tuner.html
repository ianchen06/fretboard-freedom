A phone or laptop mic works: hold the guitar close to it in a quiet room. A guitar cable into an audio interface is even steadier.

How to tune, step by step
  1. Pluck one open string firmly and let it ring. Don’t touch the other strings; rest your hand on them so they stay quiet.
  2. Find its peg. Follow the string up to the headstock. Turn the peg a little and watch which way the needle moves.
  3. Follow the arrow. ↑ Tighten raises the pitch, ↓ Loosen lowers it. Small turns: an eighth of a turn moves the high strings a lot.
  4. Always finish going up. If you’re sharp, go a little below the note and come back up. The string then holds its tuning instead of slipping flat.
  5. Go round twice. Tuning one string pulls on the neck and moves the others a little. The second time round is quick.

Electric, not plugged in? Just hold the iPad or phone close, its mic towards the strings, and pluck firmly: the tuner listens for the string’s harmonics in the room’s hiss, so it hears a string barely louder than the hiss, with no setting to change. In a hurry? Strum all six (key S) shows every string from one strum of the open strings, like a polyphonic tuner pedal: fix the ones that are out, strum again, then fine-tune string by string. Far out of tune? Pick the string with Pick a string (or keys 1–6, 1 = high e) so the tuner doesn’t mistake it for its neighbour, and it tells you how many semitones to go. In Auto the tuner follows whichever string you play. Exact is for setting intonation (the 12th-fret note against the open string): the strobe stands still when the note is spot on.
