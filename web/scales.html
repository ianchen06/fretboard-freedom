Where you played (estimated)

    CAGED in one line

    Five open chords (C, A, G, E, D) moved up the neck as barre grips tile the whole fretboard, in that order, and every scale position is built around one of them. Know the grip, and the scale shape around it follows. The letters change place with the key; their order never does.

    How the mic grades

    Sequences are checked note by note, in order, with timing against the click. In Play along every note is checked against the scale, and the first note of each bar against the chord. Grading goes by pitch, not by string: playing in the shape on the neck is up to you. Where-you-played guesses the string from each note’s sound and your hand position; it’s usually right but not always.

    Progress

    Single-shape sequences count in the Daily session’s skill map and tempo ladder, and so do one-string runs, two-shape ladders (minor pentatonic or natural minor, CAGED) and minor-pentatonic diagonals. The planner then knows what you’ve practiced here. Minutes count toward your streak.

    Keys

    P plays the sequence for you to hear. K moves the key up a 4th (C → F → B♭ …), the order the deck drills keys in. ← → pick the shape below or above. Space starts and stops.

    A week with a new scale
    1. 1
      One shape a day. Learn a shape, up and down, until the ladder moves you up twice.
    2. 2
      Link it. A two-shape ladder with yesterday’s shape: the shift is the skill.
    3. 3
      From memory. Hide the dots and play it back.
    4. 4
      Use it. Play along, staying in the new shape, then let it move every 4 bars.
