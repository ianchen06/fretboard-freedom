Pickup height

How far the strings sit above the pickups sets their volume and their tone: closer is louder and fuller, too close muddies the sound and the magnets start pulling on the strings. Set them to Gibson’s factory gap or StewMac’s starting point, then by ear.

Check the balance by ear

With the guitar plugged in, pluck each open string two or three times, as evenly as you can: each bar is how loud that string comes out. Do it on the neck pickup, then switch to the bridge pickup and do it again.

How to set it, step by step
  1. Tune up first. The gap is measured with the strings at pitch.
  2. Press the low E at the last fret and hold it there. Measure from the bottom of the string to the top of the pole piece (Gibson measures to the highest point of the pickup: the cover, or a pole screw if one is raised above it). A steel rule marked in 64ths or half-millimetres, or a string action gauge, does it.
  3. Do the same under the high E, then on the other pickup. Enter what you measured above.
  4. Turn the screw it says, a little at a time, and measure again. Hold the screwdriver firmly in the slot: a slotted screw that jumps out scratches the top.
  5. Retune and listen. These gaps are starting points. Switch between the pickups: if one is much louder, lower it or raise the other. If one string is louder or quieter than the rest, turn its pole screw: in for quieter, out for louder.
  6. Listen for the magnets. Too close, a pickup’s magnets pull on the strings: notes high up the neck on the wound strings sound wobbly or out of tune, and the sustain dies. Lower it a little.
Where the figures come from
  • Gibson factory setting: neck 3/32″ (2.4 mm), bridge 1/16″ (1.6 mm), at the last fret, the pole pieces flat; as Gibson support gives it (its factory setup sheet, quoted by Gibson staff on Gibson’s forums). Gibson’s owner’s manual: height by the two screws at either end, in the mounting ring; each string’s volume by its pole screw. Gibson’s height screws are 3-48: a full turn is 1/48″ (0.53 mm).
  • StewMac starting point: about 3/32″ under the low E and 1/16″ under the high E, at the highest fret, for humbuckers and P-90s alike, then by ear for the balance you prefer.
